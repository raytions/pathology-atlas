Organ topic

Prostate

Biopsy grading under the Gleason / ISUP system, with one large public benchmark.

Prostate cancer is graded on H&E-stained tissue by the architecture of its glands: pathologists assign Gleason patterns and summarise them as an ISUP grade group. Agreement between pathologists is imperfect, which is exactly why the task became a test bed for computational pathology.

For researchers, the practical starting point is biopsy-level grading. Labels usually exist per slide (a grade group, a Gleason score), while region masks are partial and noisy. That makes the task a natural fit for weakly supervised, multiple-instance approaches, and a useful downstream probe for pathology foundation models.

Things to check before you pick a dataset here: which institutions and scanners it covers, whether the test labels are public or held back for a challenge, and whether masks were drawn per gland or per region. The cards below show those fields; anything we have not confirmed is marked Not verified.

Prostate adenocarcinoma

  • PANDA

    Dataset

    The largest public set of prostate biopsy WSIs with grade labels, released for a 2020 Kaggle competition. Each biopsy has an ISUP grade group and Gleason score; many training slides also carry partial masks whose label scheme differs by contributing centre.

  • SICAPv2

    Dataset

    Prostate core-needle biopsy patches (512 x 512 at 10x) from 155 slides of 95 patients, each with a pixel-level Gleason grade mask, plus a primary and secondary Gleason grade per slide and a patient-level train / validation / test partition proposed by the authors.
