Organ topic

Breast

Lymph-node metastasis detection and lesion subtyping, from CAMELYON to BRACS.

Two very different problems share this page. Sentinel lymph node assessment asks whether a breast cancer has spread to the node, and how large the largest metastasis is. It is a search problem: small tumour deposits hidden in a gigapixel slide. The CAMELYON challenges turned it into the field’s first widely used WSI benchmark.

Lesion subtyping works on the breast tissue itself. Benign, atypical and malignant lesions sit on a spectrum, and the atypical categories are rare and hard to call. BRACS provides slide and region-level labels across that spectrum, with patient-level splits.

Note that many “breast” methods were only evaluated on the lymph-node task. The relation labels below tell you which is which.

Diseases

  • Breast cancer lymph-node metastasis

    Spread of breast cancer to the axillary lymph nodes, assessed first in the sentinel nodes and categorised by the size of the largest deposit (macro-metastasis, micro-metastasis, isolated tumour cells). Together with the number and location of involved nodes, it determines the pN stage.

  • Breast lesions (benign, atypical, malignant)

    The diagnostic spectrum of breast tissue lesions, from normal and benign changes through atypical (pre-cancerous) lesions to in-situ and invasive carcinoma. Some of these categories, notably the atypical lesions, are hard to tell apart on H&E.

    Dataset

    Sentinel lymph node WSIs from two Dutch hospitals with exhaustive metastasis contours, built for the first WSI challenge in computational pathology. Small, well annotated and still the default sanity check for slide-level metastasis detection.

  • CAMELYON17

    Dataset

    A five-centre extension of CAMELYON16 that moves from single slides to patients: slides are grouped into artificial patients and scored by pN stage. Useful for testing robustness across centres and scanners.
